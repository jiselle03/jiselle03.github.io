## Design Context

### Users
Hiring managers, engineering leads, recruiters, and peers evaluating Jiselle Liu as a product-minded fullstack engineer with strong UI/UX judgment. They need to understand technical range, product judgment, interface craft, and proof of recent product work quickly.

### Brand Personality
Direct, capable, product-minded. The portfolio should feel memorable and intentionally designed without reading as gimmicky, over-animated, or generated from a generic template.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a polished engineering portfolio direction: editorial structure, crisp typography, restrained motion, and selective visual details that show UI/UX taste. Recent work should be framed in NDA-safe terms around a tutoring platform, an AI hiring platform, and Hum, a personal fitness/recovery tracker. The recent-work positioning should communicate product development range: owning product direction and shipping the build, with real attention to UI/UX and end-user interaction. Earlier work can mention freight payments, lending, e-commerce, and XR commerce at a broad category level only. Forward engineering can be presented as a capability: helping companies streamline workflows with pragmatic AI, without implying a specific recent engagement. The preferred current stack is TypeScript, Next.js, React, tRPC, Drizzle, PostgreSQL, Tailwind, OpenAI, and Vitest. Additional experience can mention Vue, Ruby on Rails, Go, Express, MongoDB, React Query, Playwright, Claude Code, and Codex without positioning them as the primary stack. Editorial/writing background can point to the Writing link when it supports product, copy, or UX judgment.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with proof of work: project outcomes, technical decisions, and role clarity should be visible in the first meaningful viewport.
2. Keep the runtime lean: ship mostly static content with minimal client JavaScript unless an interaction clearly improves the experience.
3. Make the craft visible: typography, layout, responsiveness, focus states, and micro-interactions should demonstrate frontend judgment.
4. Avoid portfolio cliches: no generic card grid, purple gradients, decorative metrics, or vague "passionate developer" copy.
5. Preserve accessibility as part of the brand: semantic HTML, keyboard support, contrast, reduced motion, and resilient mobile layouts are non-negotiable.
